\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}
% ==============================================================================

This appendix describes in full detail the construction of the conjoint analysis
question included in the Global Justice questionnaire. The conjoint presents
respondents with pairs of stylised societal scenarios and asks them to choose
between them. Each scenario is characterised by eight underlying parameters
(Section~\ref{sec:parameters}) and displayed through seven features
(Sections~\ref{sec:temperature}--\ref{sec:barcharts}). The income distributions underlying the features
are derived from \citet{Bothe2026} and \citet{Chancel2026}; temperature
projections rely on the emission pathways from \citet{Chancel2026}. All monetary
quantities are expressed in EUR PPP 2025 per adult per year unless otherwise
noted.

% ==============================================================================
\section{Scenario nomenclature}
\label{sec:naming}
% ==============================================================================

A scenario is characterised by four structural dimensions:

\begin{description}
  \item[Hours (or class).] The average weekly working hours converge to a
    2100 target corresponding to one of: \scenario{S15k}, \scenario{S45k},
    \scenario{S} (= \scenario{S60k}), \scenario{B} (= \scenario{S} at 2100, constant-growth productivity),
    \scenario{B90k} (formerly \scenario{M}), \scenario{B120k} (formerly \scenario{W}), \scenario{P} (from
    shortest to longest; see Table~\ref{tab:hours}).
    \scenario{B} has the same 35h/60k target as \scenario{S} but uses
    constant-growth productivity at 2035 (see Section~\ref{sec:MC}).
  \item[Scope.] The redistribution configuration:
    \scenario{C} = Convergence (both national and global);
    \scenario{G} = Global only;
    \scenario{N} = National only;
    \scenario{I} = Inequality (neither).
  \item[Sectoral change.] \scenario{1} = no sectoral change (stable public
    services, current food system); \\\scenario{2} = structural change (expanded
    public services, reduced beef and flights).
  \item[Decarbonization.] \scenario{SD} = slow; \scenario{ID} = intermediate;
    \scenario{FD} = fast.
\end{description}

\noindent By convention, the scope is embedded in the hours label (e.g.\
\scenario{SC45k2\_FD} for $30\,\text{h}/\text{wk}$, Convergence, structural
change, fast decarbonization). The default benchmark is
\scenario{SC2\_FD} ($35\,\text{h}$, Convergence, structural change, fast
decarbonization).

\section{Eight underlying parameters}
\label{sec:parameters}
% ==============================================================================

Each respondent profile is defined by one parameter representing the
respondent themselves and seven policy-relevant parameters:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Individual income}: the respondent's household monthly cash
    income and whether they live as a couple. The annual individual income is
    $y_0 = \text{HH\_monthly} \times 12 / k$, where $k = 2$ if couple, $k = 1$
    otherwise.

  \item \textbf{Decarbonization}: Slow (\scenario{SD}), Intermediate
    (\scenario{ID}), Fast (\scenario{FD}), corresponding to IEA CPS, STEPS
    and NZE pathways respectively. The income cost at 2035 is 0\,\% (SD), 1\,\% (ID) or 3\,\% (FD). % TODO put the cost info elsewhere

  \item \textbf{Working hours}: each scenario class converges to a 2100 GDP-per-capita
    target (labels 29/30/35/40/45, i.e.\ 30/45/60/90/120\,k\EUR; see Table~\ref{tab:hours}).
    The corresponding weekly hours \emph{worked in 2035} are 28/32/36/40/44, evenly spaced
    around the \scenario{B90k} baseline (40\,h) in steps of 4.

  \item \textbf{National redistribution}: current level, or National redistribution (\scenario{N}).

  \item \textbf{Global redistribution}: current level, or Global redistribution (\scenario{N}).

  \item \textbf{Public services}: stable (20\,\% of GNI) or increased
    (+3 percentage points of GNI, financed by a flat-rate tax).

  \item \textbf{Beef and flights}: no change; $-60\,\%$ beef; $-50\,\%$ air travel; both changes.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The full design yields $5 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 2
\times 4 = 480$ unique scenarios.

\section{Income distribution data sources}
\label{sec:data}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{National secondary income from \texorpdfstring{\citet{Bothe2026}}{Bothe et al.}}

The \citet{Bothe2026} simulation provides, for each country $c$, gpercentile $p$
and year $t$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\text{sdiinc}_{c,p,t}$: the income share of bracket $p$ in country $c$;
  \item $\text{nni}_{c,t}$: national net income per adult (EUR PPP 2025);
  \item $\text{diff}_{c,t}$: a normalising scalar;
  \item $\text{ypt}_{c,p,t}$: the global income tax (GIT) paid;
  \item $\text{div}_t$: the uniform global dividend received by every adult.
\end{itemize}

The resulting per-adult full income is:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:income}
  \text{income}_{c,p,t} =
    \underbrace{\frac{\text{sdiinc}_{c,p,t}\cdot\text{nni}_{c,t}}{\text{diff}_{c,t}}}_{\text{yp\_recomp: national secondary income, pre-GIT}}
    - \text{ypt}_{c,p,t} + \text{div}_t .
\end{equation}

$\text{yp\_recomp}$ is the national secondary income \emph{after} all national
taxes and transfers but \emph{before} the global income tax. All four
redistribution scopes are therefore defined as:
\begin{align}
  \text{SC}_{c,p,t} &= \text{income}_{c,p,t}
    = \text{yp\_recomp} - \text{ypt} + \text{div}, \label{eq:SC} \\
  \text{SN}_{c,p,t} &= (\text{SC}_{c,p,t} - \text{GR}_t)\,
    \bigl(G^{SI_0}_{c}/G^{SC}_{c}\bigr), \label{eq:SN} \\
  \text{SI}_{c,p,t} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\, \text{PI}_{c,p,t}
    \quad (\text{in 2100; see Section~\ref{sec:2100}}), \label{eq:SI} \\
  \text{SG}_{c,p,t} &= \text{SI}_{c,p,t}\,\bigl(1 - \tau^G(\text{SI}_{c,p,t})\bigr). \label{eq:SG}
\end{align}
where $G^{SC}_{c},G^{SI_0}_{c}$ are the gp-weighted mean incomes of the SC and SI ($=\tfrac12$PI)
distributions and $\text{GR}=\text{div}-\text{ypt}$ is the net global transfer (so
$\text{SC}-\text{GR}=\text{yp\_recomp}$). The N scope rescales the nationally-redistributed income
to the no-global GDP level $G^{SI_0}$. The G scope keeps the SI (no-redistribution) within-country
shape and applies a \emph{global} tax schedule that is a function of N (national, pre-global) income,
$\tau^G(\cdot)$: at world percentile $g$ the N income $\text{SN}^{World}_g$ faces the rate
$(\text{SN}^{World}_g-\text{SC}^{World}_g)/\text{SN}^{World}_g$ (negative for poor incomes, positive
for rich). This schedule is then \emph{evaluated at each unit's own I (SI) income}. Because $\tau^G$
is read off the \emph{world} distribution, the identity $\text{SG}=\text{SI}\,(\text{SC}/\text{SN})$
holds at the world level but not within a single country.

\attentionbox{%
  \citet{Bothe2026} provide a single SC simulation. The SC1 and SC2 variants
  (sectoral change off/on) and all decarbonization paces (SD/ID/FD) share the
  same income distribution. The differences between them are modelled
  \emph{explicitly} by us through (a) an income-cost multiplier for
  decarbonization (Section~\ref{sec:decarb}) and (b) a flat-rate PS tax for
  structural change (Section~\ref{sec:pstax}).
}

\subsection{Cash income for Italy 2025}

The conjoint uses \emph{gross cash income}: primary income at market prices,
net of all taxes (including VAT) and net of imputed rents and retained
earnings, but \emph{gross} of capital consumption. This corresponds to what a
respondent would recognise as their disposable income plus employer social
contributions, comparable across scenarios.

Starting from \citet{FisherGethin2023} data for
Italy:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cash2025}
  \text{cash}_{p} =
    \bigl( a^\text{pre}_p - \tau^\text{dir}_p - \tau^\text{soc}_p
           - \tau^\text{ind}_p + g^\text{soc}_p \bigr)\,\text{NNI}
    - r_e\, \kappa_p\, \text{NNI}
    - r_h\, H_p\, \text{NNI}
    + \kappa_p\, \text{CFC}
    - \text{ypt}_p ,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $a^\text{pre}_p$ = normalised pretax income share at percentile $p$;
  \item $\tau^\text{dir}_p$, $\tau^\text{soc}_p$, $\tau^\text{ind}_p$ = direct
    taxes (PIT + wealth + CIT), social contributions, and indirect taxes (VAT),
    each normalised by mean pretax income;
  \item $g^\text{soc}_p$ = government cash transfers;
  \item $r_e = 0.50 \times \bar{m}$ = retained-earnings deduction rate ($\bar{m}$
    = WID corporate profit retention ratio for Italy);
  \item $r_h = 0.035$ = imputed-rent yield on net housing wealth
    \citep{Garbinti2021};
  \item $H_p$ = normalised housing-wealth allocation at percentile $p$
    (interpolated from the 2014 French wealth-composition data of
    \citealt{Garbinti2021});
  \item $\kappa_p$ = capital-income share at percentile $p$;
  \item $\text{CFC}$ = capital consumption per adult (so as to gross up from NNI
    to GNI for the capital share);
  \item $\text{ypt}_p$ = GIT tax already paid in 2025.
\end{itemize}

We enforce monotonicity on the bottom-99\,\% of the distribution by sorting
values in ascending order, mirroring the procedure in the main distributional
series. All values are expressed in EUR PPP 2025.

\attentionbox{%
  $\tau^\text{ind}$ (VAT) is subtracted because the \citet{FisherGethin2023} pretax income is
  at market prices (inclusive of indirect taxes). Subtracting VAT converts to
  factor-cost NNI, to which cash incomes sum. Omitting this step would
  overstate cash income.
}

\section{IT distributions at 2035}
\label{sec:it2035}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Base distribution: IT35}

Let $\text{IT35}_p$ denote the gross cash income distribution for Italy at
2035 under \scenario{SC1\_SD} (slow decarbonization, no structural change).
It serves as the universal base from which all 2035 IT scenario distributions
are derived. We compute it by rescaling the 2035 \citet{Bothe2026} income distribution
to cash units using the 2025 individual cash-to-income ratio:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:IT35}
  \text{IT35}_p = \text{income}_{IT,p,2035} \times
    \frac{\text{cash}_{p}}{\text{income}_{IT,p,2025}} .
\end{equation}
This per-percentile ratio preserves the distributional shape while anchoring the
level to the respondent-observable 2025 cash income.

\subsection{GR35: the 2035 net global redistribution transfer}

The per-percentile net global redistribution transfer in 2035 is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cashGR35}
  \text{cash\_GR35}_p = \bigl(\text{div}_{2035} - \text{ypt}_{p,2035}\bigr)
    \times \frac{\text{cash}_{p}}{\text{income}_{IT,p,2025}} .
\end{equation}
$\text{cash\_GR35}_p$ is positive for low-income individuals (net GIT
receivers) and negative for high-income individuals (net GIT contributors).

\attentionbox{%
  Monotonicity is \emph{not} enforced on \col{cash\_GR35}. Unlike income
  distributions, which must be weakly increasing, the net GIT transfer is
  mechanically decreasing with income (higher earners pay more than they
  receive). Sorting it would scramble the distributional signal.
}

\subsection{Decarbonization income factor}
\label{sec:decarb}

We model the income cost of decarbonization as a flat multiplicative factor
applied to all 2035 distributions:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:decarb}
  \delta = \begin{cases}
    1.00 & \text{(SD)} \\
    0.99 & \text{(ID)} \\
    0.97 & \text{(FD)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This captures a $-3\,\%$ income penalty for fast decarbonization relative to
slow, consistent with the income-side impact of accelerated investment in the
energy transition.

\attentionbox{%
  The factor $\delta$ is applied \emph{on top of} the \citet{Bothe2026} income distribution.
  \citet{Bothe2026} report the same income distribution regardless of the decarbonization
  pace (the SD, ID and FD variants differ only in emissions and temperature). The
  $\delta$ multiplier is therefore an explicit parametric cost not already embedded
  in the simulation.
}

\subsection{Public-services flat-rate tax}
\label{sec:pstax}

When public services are expanded (\scenario{food=2}), the additional expenditure
is financed by a flat-rate tax at rate
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:pstax}
  \tau_\text{PS} = \frac{(s_{2035} - s_{2025})\times \bar{g}_{2035}}{\bar{\text{cash}}_{2035}},
\end{equation}
where $s_t$ = share of educ+health+PS spending in GNE (from \citet{Chancel2026} sheet
\texttt{G5s}), $\bar{g}_{2035}$ = per-adult GNE at 2035 (\texttt{G0p}), and
$\bar{\text{cash}}_{2035}$ = average of IT35. For Italy: $\tau_\text{PS}
\approx 4.9\,\%$.

\subsection{Scenario-specific 2035 IT distributions}

All 2035 IT columns are derived from IT35 using the following formulas, where
$\delta_\text{FD} = 0.97$ and $\psi = 1 - \tau_\text{PS}$:

\begin{align}
  \scenario{IT35}  &= \text{IT35}_p  &&\text{(SC1\_SD: base)} \\
  \scenario{SCmat} &= \text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD}  &&\text{(SC1\_FD)} \\
  \scenario{SC}    &= \text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi &&\text{(SC2\_FD)} \\
  \scenario{PC}    &= 1.15\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD}  &&\text{(PC1\_FD)} \\
  \scenario{PI}    &= 1.4\,\text{IT25}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD}   &&\text{(PI1\_FD)} \\
  \scenario{SC45k} &= (G_{2025}/G_{2035})\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi \\
  \scenario{SC15k} &= 0.9\,(G_{2025}/G_{2035})\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi \\
  \scenario{SI}    &= (G^{PI}_{2035}/G_{2025})\,(h^{SC}_{2035}/h^{PI}_{2035})\,\text{IT25}_p
                       \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi \\
  \scenario{SN}    &= (\text{IT35}_p - \text{cash\_GR35}_p)\,(G^{SI_0}/\overline{\text{SC}})
                       \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi \\
  \scenario{SG}    &= (\overline{\text{SC}}/G^{SI_0})\,(\text{SI0}_p + \text{cash\_GR35}_p)
                       \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi \\
  \scenario{B90kC}   &= \Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(B90kC2\_FD: 40h baseline + GIT; formerly \scenario{MC})} \\
  \scenario{B120kC}  &= \tfrac{44}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(B120kC2\_FD: 45h class, 44h worked; at 2100 = PC; formerly \scenario{WC})} \\
  \scenario{B90kMat} &= \Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD}
                       &&\text{(B90kC1\_FD: no PS tax; formerly \scenario{MCmat})} \\
  \scenario{B}       &= \tfrac{36}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(B2\_FD: 35h class, 36h worked; at 2100 = SC; formerly \scenario{M60k})} \\
  \scenario{Bmat}    &= \tfrac{36}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD}
                       &&\text{(B1\_FD: B without PS tax)} \\
  \scenario{Bbeef}   &= \scenario{B}
                       &&\text{(B2\_FD, beef focus; income = B)} \\
  \scenario{Bflights}&= \scenario{B}
                       &&\text{(B2\_FD, flights focus; income = B)} \\
  \scenario{B45kC}   &= \tfrac{32}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(B45kC2\_FD: 30h class, 32h worked; formerly \scenario{MC45k})} \\
  \scenario{B30kC}   &= \tfrac{28}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(29h class, 28h worked; formerly \scenario{MC30k})} \\
  \scenario{B15kC}   &= \tfrac{24}{40}\,\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p \cdot \delta_\text{FD} \cdot \psi
                       &&\text{(28h class, 24h worked; formerly \scenario{MC15k})}
\end{align}
% B90k is the 40h baseline; every other B class is B90k × (worked-hours/40), worked hours 28/32/36/40/44.

\noindent where $G_{t}$ denotes Italy's per-capita GDP at year $t$ (\citealt{Chancel2026} \texttt{A0p};
$G^{PI}_{2035}$ is the PI per-capita GDP, \texttt{A0pi}); $h^{SC}_{2035},h^{PI}_{2035}$ are
per-capita economic labour hours under SC/PI (\citealt{Chancel2026} \texttt{E0h}/\texttt{E0k});
$\text{SI0}_p=(G^{PI}_{2035}/G_{2025})(h^{SC}/h^{PI})\,\text{IT25}_p$ is the SI base, with
$\overline{\text{SC}},G^{SI_0}$ the gp-weighted means of the SC and SI bases; and $\Phi$ is the
B90k income scale factor defined in Section~\ref{sec:MC}. \scenario{B90k} (40\,h) is the baseline:
every other B class equals $\Phi\,\text{IT35}_p$ times its 2035 worked-hours ratio (28/32/36/40/44 over 40).

\attentionbox{%
  \scenario{PI} uses IT25 (2025 cash income) as its base, not IT35, because the
  PI scenario does not undergo the SC productivity and redistribution transition
  between 2025 and 2035. It grows from the current distribution by a flat
  GDP-per-capita factor of 1.4. By contrast, \scenario{SC} and its variants
  start from IT35, which already embeds the \citet{Bothe2026} SC income structure at 2035.
}

\attentionbox{%
  \scenario{SI} uses IT25 as its base, scaled to the no-global GDP level: the PI per-capita
  GDP adjusted for SC's shorter working hours, $(G^{PI}_{2035}/G_{2025})(h^{SC}/h^{PI})$. It
  preserves the current within-country shape (no redistribution). \scenario{SN} and \scenario{SG}
  then sit at the same no-global level $G^{SI_0}$ (SN) and the converged level $\overline{\text{SC}}$
  (SG): \scenario{SN} rescales the SC-redistributed income $(\text{IT35}-\text{GR35})$ to $G^{SI_0}$,
  and \scenario{SG} rescales the global-transfer-augmented SI base $(\text{SI0}+\text{cash\_GR35})$
  to $\overline{\text{SC}}$.
}

\subsection{B90k scenario: moderate convergence (formerly MC)}
\label{sec:MC}

The \scenario{B90k} scenario (formerly \scenario{M}/\scenario{MC}) holds per-\emph{worker}
working hours constant at their 2025 level (40\,h) and is the baseline of the B family. Its
2035 income scale relative to IT35 is:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:MCscale}
  \Phi = \frac{(1+g)^{10}}{\omega_{2035} \cdot \phi_{2035}}, \qquad g = 1\,\%,
\end{equation}
where $(1+g)^{10}$ is the cumulative 2025--2035 income growth of the B scenarios at a flat
yearly rate $g = 1\,\%$ (this replaces the former practice of using the average 2025--2100
productivity rate $\bar{g}^{10}$, with $\bar{g} = (125/\phi_{2025})^{1/75}$, which front-loads
SC's high near-term growth), $\phi_{2035} = \phi_{2035}/\phi_{2025}$ is the actual SC productivity
growth 2025--2035 (from \citet{Chancel2026} sheet \texttt{F0a}), and $\omega_{2035} =
h^{pw}_{2035}/h^{pw}_{2025}$ is the SC per-\emph{worker}-hours change 2025--2035 (sheet
\texttt{E0a}).

At 2100, \scenario{B90k} income is a fixed multiple of \scenario{SC}:
$\text{B90kC}_{2100} = 1.5 \times \text{SC}_{2100}$ (the 90k GDP-per-capita target, vs SC's 60k),
applied uniformly to IT and the world distribution. The 2100 hours ladder uses fixed
GDP-target coefs (target$/60$) for all hours levels (see Table~\ref{tab:coefs2100}); the
per-worker hours ratio from sheet \texttt{E0a} is used only for the 2035 income scale (below).

\attentionbox{%
  In the SC scenario, productivity growth is front-loaded (peaking around 2080
  before declining), so the actual SC growth $\phi_{2035}$ can exceed the flat-rate
  numerator $(1.01)^{10}\approx 1.105$. In that case $\Phi < 1$, meaning \scenario{B90k} income at 2035 is
  \emph{lower} than SC income — a plausible result because SC extracts peak
  productivity via its 35\,h week while \scenario{B90k} works 40\,h at a lower per-hour
  frontier.
}

\section{Income distributions at 2100}
\label{sec:2100}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Income concept and rescaling}

At 2100, within-country income comparisons across scenarios and between
countries are best made using ``full income'' (national secondary income
inclusive of all public services), rather than cash income. However, to keep
the 2100 income comparable with the 2035 cash income entered by the respondent
(and shown in the IT35/SC columns), we rescale by a \emph{single scalar}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ratioIT}
  r_\text{IT} = \frac{\overline{\text{cash}}_{IT,2025}}{\overline{\text{income}}_{IT,2025}},
\end{equation}
where both averages are taken over the 127-bracket Italy 2025 distribution
weighted by bracket widths. In the data $r_\text{IT} \approx 0.69$.

This ratio is applied uniformly across all gpercentiles and all countries.
The resulting 2100 distributions therefore represent cash-income-equivalent
magnitudes, stripped of the within-country variation in the cash-to-income
ratio. The consistency check is: $r_\text{IT} \times \bar{y}_{IT,2100}
\approx r_\text{IT} \times \bar{y}_{World,2100}$ in the SC scenario (since SC
achieves near-complete global convergence by 2100), which holds in the data.

\attentionbox{%
  The decision to use a \emph{scalar} $r_\text{IT}$ for 2100 (rather than the
  per-percentile ratio used for 2035) is deliberate. For 2035, the per-percentile
  ratio is needed to preserve distributional comparability with the cash income
  entered by the respondent. For 2100, cross-country and cross-scenario
  comparability is the priority, which requires a \emph{uniform} rescaling so
  that the shape of the 2100 world distribution reflects true inequality rather
  than the Italy-specific cash-to-income heteroscedasticity.
}

\subsection{IT 2100 distributions}

For Italy at 2100, in raw full-income units (before $r_\text{IT}$):
\begin{align}
  \scenario{SC}_{IT,2100} &= \text{income}_{IT,p,2100}
    \quad (\text{\citealt{Bothe2026} SC: GIT+dividend}) \\
  \scenario{SN}_{IT,2100} &= (\text{income}_{IT,p,2100} - \text{div}_{2100})\,
    (G^{SI_0}_{IT}/G^{SC}_{IT}) \quad (\text{(SC$-$GR)} \times G^{SI_0}/G^{SC}) \\
  \scenario{PI}_{IT,2100} &= \text{yp\_recomp}_{IT,p,2025} \times g^\text{PI}_{IT}
    \quad (g^\text{PI} = A0\text{pi}_{2100}/A0\text{pi}_{2025}) \\
  \scenario{SI}_{IT,2100} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\scenario{PI}_{IT,2100} \\
  \scenario{SG}_{IT,2100} &= \scenario{SI}_{IT,2100}\,\bigl(1 - \tau^G(\scenario{SI}_{IT,2100})\bigr)
    \quad (\tau^G \text{ a function of N income, read at IT's SI income})
\end{align}

For all hours variants, apply the coefs from Table~\ref{tab:coefs2100}
to the \scenario{SC} distribution:
\begin{equation}
  \text{X}_{IT,2100} = c_X \times \scenario{SC}_{IT,2100}.
\end{equation}
Each weekly-hours level maps to a fixed 2100 GDP-per-capita target (k\EUR, with global
redistribution), so its coef is simply target$/60$: \scenario{B30k} (29h, 30k)$\to 0.5$,
\scenario{B45k} (30h, 45k)$\to 0.75$, \scenario{B} (35h, 60k)$\to 1.0$, \scenario{B90k}
(40h, 90k)$\to 1.5$, \scenario{B120k} (45h, 120k)$\to 2.0$.
\scenario{B} (formerly \scenario{M60k}) and \scenario{Bbeef}/\scenario{Bflights}/\scenario{Bmat}
all equal \scenario{SC} at 2100 (B reaches the SC level at 2100).

\subsection{World 2100 distributions}

The world distribution is built by pooling all (country, gpercentile) cells,
weighting each cell by $w_{c,p} = \Delta_p \times \text{Pop}_c$, sorting by
income value, and binning into 100 equal-population global percentiles. Cells
with non-positive or non-finite income are dropped. Gaps in the resulting
100-grid are filled by linear interpolation.

Populations used follow whether the scenario has global redistribution (GIT): $\text{Pop}^{SC}$
(\citealt{Chancel2026} sheet \texttt{Z0a}, $\approx 9.41$\,B in 2100, Convergence) for SC, SG, SC45k, SC15k, \scenario{B90k}
and PC; and $\text{Pop}^{PI}$ (\texttt{Z0b}, $\approx 10.18$\,B, Inequality) for PI, SI and SN.

\attentionbox{%
  PC inherits the SC world distribution scaled by 2.0, using SC population.
  A more rigorous treatment would use the UN-medium population for PC (as
  \citealt{Chancel2026} recommend), but the implied distributional error is small
  since country-level growth differences matter more than aggregate population.
}

\subsection{Output format for ineq\_2100}

The file \texttt{ineq\_2100.csv} contains 21 income brackets:
$p_0p_5,\, p_5p_{10},\, \ldots,\, p_{90}p_{95},\, p_{95}p_{99},\, p_{99}p_{100}$.
Each IT bracket is the gp-width-weighted mean over the 127-grid; each World
bracket is the unweighted mean over the corresponding 100-grid bins. All values
are rounded to the nearest integer EUR.

\section{Temperature at 2100}
\label{sec:temperature}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Base temperature}

Temperature depends on GDP (which encodes working hours and global redistribution),
decarbonization pace, and sectoral change. For scenarios present in \citet{Chancel2026}'s
\texttt{Emission\_Output} workbook, we use $T_\text{final}$ from
\texttt{chancel\_temp2100\_completed.csv}. For scenarios not modelled by
\citet{Chancel2026} (SI, SN, SG, \scenario{B90k} and their variants), we predict temperature using
a GDP-based no-emissions regression estimated on the 24 scenarios observed by \citet{Chancel2026}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:noem}
  T = \beta_0 + \beta_1 G + \beta_2 \mathbf{1}_\text{ID} + \beta_3 \mathbf{1}_\text{SD}
    + \beta_4 G\,\mathbf{1}_\text{ID} + \beta_5 G\,\mathbf{1}_\text{SD}
    + \beta_6 c\,\mathbf{1}_\text{FD} + \beta_7 c\,\mathbf{1}_\text{ID}
    + \beta_8 c\,\mathbf{1}_\text{SD} + \varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $G$ = total 2100 GDP (T\EUR), $\mathbf{1}_d$ = indicator for
decarbonization pace $d$, $c = \mathbf{1}(\text{structural change} = 1)$,
and $\varepsilon$ is the residual. Adjusted $R^2 \approx 0.997$.

The total 2100 GDP is computed as $G = g_\text{pc} \times P$, where $g_\text{pc}$
is GDP per capita from Table~\ref{tab:hours} and $P$ is the 2100 population (9.41\,B
for SC family, 10.18\,B for PI/no-GIT at 45h).

\subsection{Temperature adjustments for beef, flights and public services}

Following \citet{Chancel2026}, once a ``canonical'' modality is fixed, additive
corrections are applied:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Beef reduction}: $-0.24$\,°C.
  \item \textbf{Flight reduction}: $-0.155$\,°C.
  \item \textbf{Expanded public services} (relative to no expansion):
    $\pm\,0.0004875 \times (67.25 + 2.06\,g_\text{pc})$\,°C, where $g_\text{pc}$
    is in k\EUR/adult. This formula is derived from the regression of fossil
    emissions on GDP per capita across SC scenarios (Section~17 of the
    \texttt{questionnaire\_legacy.R} script).
\end{itemize}

The canonical modality is chosen as follows \citep[method\_questionnaire.md]{Chancel2026}:
\begin{description}
  \item[SD and ID:] Fix the change according to the public-services choice
    (stable $\to$ 1, increased $\to$ 2). Adjust for beef and flights relative to
    the canonical co-variation with structural change.
  \item[FD:] Fix the change according to the beef choice (no reduction $\to$ 1,
    beef reduced $\to$ 2). Land-use effects dominate at fast decarbonization.
    Adjust for flights and public services (the latter already captured via
    the sectoral-change dummy in equation~\ref{eq:noem}).
\end{description}

\attentionbox{%
  Temperature is rounded to the nearest 0.1\,°C. In the no-emissions regression
  the maximum absolute deviation from the temperatures observed by \citet{Chancel2026} is 0.1\,°C
  (by construction: the model was selected to guarantee this on the training
  set). For extrapolated scenarios (SI/SN/SG/MC) the true error is unknown but
  bounded by the regression's generalization uncertainty.
}

% ==============================================================================
\section{Own income at 2035}
\label{sec:ownincome}
% ==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute the respondent's annual individual income:
    $y_0 = y_\text{HH}\times 12 / k$.

  \item Find the respondent's gpercentile $p^*$ in the IT25 distribution by
    linear interpolation on the 127-bracket cumulative distribution.

  \item Retrieve their 2035 income $\hat{y}_{2035}$ by interpolating the
    appropriate scenario column of \texttt{ineq\_IT\_2035.csv} at $p^*$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The scenario column is determined by the user's working hours,
redistribution scope, decarbonization and public-services choices (after
applying $\delta$ and $\psi$ corrections to the exported base column).

For hours levels not equal to 35h, a non-convergence scope column is built from
the 35h base of \emph{that same scope}, so it keeps the scope's own
within-country shape (national for N, inequality for G) rather than the converged
SC shape. The 35h scope base is rescaled to the target hours class $x$ by
\begin{align}
  \scenario{N}_{x} &= \scenario{SN}\,\frac{\bar{y}^{I}_{x}}{\bar{\scenario{SN}}}, &
  \scenario{G}_{x} &= (\scenario{SI}+\text{cash\_GR35})\,\frac{\bar{y}^{C}_{x}}{\bar{\scenario{SI}}},
\end{align}
where $\bar{y}^{I}_{x}$ ($\bar{y}^{C}_{x}$) is the gp-weighted mean of the
inequality (convergence) distribution at hours class $x \in
\{\text{S15k},\text{B45kC},\text{B90k},\text{P}\}$, and the bars denote 35h-column
weighted means in \texttt{ineq\_IT\_2035.csv}. (At $x = $ 35h the ratio is 1 and
the scope base is returned unchanged.)

\attentionbox{%
  The IT25 distribution may not be monotone over the full 127 gpercentile range
  due to imputation noise in the top-1\,\% brackets. The percentile search is
  therefore applied only to the 99-bracket bottom distribution; above
  gpercentile 99, the last bracket's income is returned.
}

% ==============================================================================
\section{2100 income bar charts}
\label{sec:barcharts}
% ==============================================================================

The \emph{national incomes} chart shows the IT 2100 distribution in 21 brackets
and the \emph{global incomes} chart shows both the IT and the World 2100
distributions. All values are cash-income-equivalent (scaled by $r_\text{IT}$).

For hours variants not equal to 35h, the ineq\_2100 distribution for the
given redistribution scope is scaled by:
\begin{equation}
  c_\text{hours} = \frac{\bar{W}_\text{hours}}{\bar{W}_\text{SC}},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{W}$ denotes the bracket-weighted world average from
\texttt{ineq\_2100.csv}. This ratio equals the hours coef from
Table~\ref{tab:coefs2100} for the GIT+national scope, and generalises to other
scopes by the same proportionality.

\attentionbox{%
  The bar charts display \emph{average} income within each bracket. The top
  bracket ($p_{99}p_{100}$) typically shows a very steep increase: in the SC
  scenario this bracket is compressed by redistribution; in the PI/SI scenarios
  it can be an order of magnitude above the median. When displaying these charts
  in the questionnaire, consider truncating or log-scaling the $y$-axis to
  avoid the top bracket dominating the visual.
}

\section{Consistency checks and known limitations}
\label{sec:checks}
% ==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SN vs SI}: both sit at the no-global GDP level $G^{SI_0}$ (same
    average) but differ in shape: SN starts from the nationally-redistributed
    income (IT35$-$GR35, i.e.\ SC's compressed shape) and SI from the 2025
    distribution (IT25). The wedge between them at intermediate percentiles is the
    effect of national redistribution alone.

  \item \textbf{SG vs SI}: SG applies a \emph{global} income-tax schedule
    $\tau^G(\cdot)$ --- a function of N income, read off the world
    $\scenario{SN}\!\to\!\scenario{SC}$ distributions and evaluated at each unit's I (SI) income ---
    to the inequality base ($\scenario{SG}=\scenario{SI}\,(1-\tau^G(\scenario{SI}))$). It keeps the
    no-national-redistribution shape of SI but reallocates income across countries as the global
    transfer does worldwide: a given income faces the same rate everywhere, negative
    (subsidy) at low incomes and positive (tax) at high ones. Because $\tau^G$ is global, the
    identity $\scenario{SG}=\scenario{SI}\,(\scenario{SC}/\scenario{SN})$ holds at the world level
    but \emph{not} within IT (IT's percentiles borrow the global income$\to$rate map, not IT's own).
    Hours variants of the G scope scale the base SG by the same hours coef as the other scopes
    (the income distribution, and hence the rate schedule, scales with the economy).

  \item \textbf{IT\_SC = World\_SC at 2100}: Under SC, countries converge to
    approximately the same per-capita income by 2100, so Italy's 2100 distribution
    and the world's 2100 distribution should have similar averages. This is
    confirmed in the data ($\bar{y}_{IT,SC} \approx \bar{y}_{World,SC}$ after
    applying $r_\text{IT}$).

  \item \textbf{PI base for 2100}: The PI base uses $\text{yp\_recomp}_{2025}$
    (pre-GIT national secondary income) rather than $\text{income}_{2025}$ (which
    includes the 2025 GIT dividend). Using the post-dividend income would
    overstate PI incomes for net GIT receivers and understate them for net
    contributors.

  \item \textbf{No PI2 scenarios}: The PI scenario does not model sectoral change
    in \citet{Chancel2026}'s data ($\text{sectoral\_change} = 0$ for all PI variants,
    regardless of the public-services choice). This is a limitation: PI+expanded
    PS is computed using structural change $= 0$, so the temperature does not
    reflect any emissions benefit from sectoral reallocation.

  \item \textbf{B90k temperature}: \scenario{B90k} is not in \citet{Chancel2026}'s emission output
    workbook. Its temperature is predicted by the no-emissions regression, which
    may not generalise well to the \scenario{B90k} GDP level ($1.5 \times 60 \times 9.41 \approx 847$\,T\EUR),
    which lies between SC (565\,T\EUR) and PC (1129\,T\EUR) but has a distinct productivity path.
\end{enumerate}

    B45kC, B120kC, B90kI, B90kN, B90kG, B, BG, BN, BI, Bbeef, Bflight, B90kC\_SD).
    Income in EUR PPP 2025/adult/yr.

    127 rows $\times$ additional columns: all 6 hours classes $\times$ 4 scopes + standalone
    columns SCmat, B90kMat, Bmat, Bbeef, Bflights + B90k sub-target variants

  \item[\texttt{ineq2\_IT\_2035.csv}, \texttt{ineq2\_IT\_2035\_full.csv}]
    Same as \texttt{ineq\_IT\_2035(\_full).csv} but the B scenarios use a $2\,\%$ yearly growth
    for 2025--2035 instead of $1\,\%$. Every B-named column is scaled by
    $(1.02)^{10}/(1.01)^{10}\approx 1.10$; non-B columns are identical.

  \item[\texttt{ineq\_2100.csv}]
    21 rows (brackets) $\times$ columns (bracket label + IT and World for each
    of the selected scenarios, including B90kC, B120kC). Income in EUR PPP 2025/adult/yr $\times$ $r_\text{IT}$.

  \item[\texttt{ineq\_2100\_full.csv}]
    21 rows $\times$ additional columns: all 6 hours classes $\times$ 4 scopes + SCmat +
    B90k sub-target variants B\{45k/30k/15k\}\{C/G/N/I\} + Bbeef, Bflights, Bmat (= SC at 2100, since B=SC at 2100) +
    B120k\{C/G/N/I\} (= P\{C/G/N/I\} at 2100; formerly W\{C/G/N/I\}).

  \item[\texttt{temp100.csv}]
    54 rows: 30 \citet{Chancel2026} scenarios + 24 new rows for SI/SN/SG/\scenario{B90k} at all
    decarb $\times$ food combinations. Columns: scenario, gdp (T\EUR),
    sectoral\_change, decarb, temp\_final (°C).

  \item[\texttt{conjoint\_constants.csv}]
    Key-value rows covering: $r_\text{IT}$, $\tau_\text{PS}$, decarbonization
    factors, temperature adjustment coefficients, GDP per capita by hours $\times$
    GIT, population projections, no-emissions regression coefficients, and average
    incomes from both distribution files (now with B90k/B/B120k column names).
\end{description}
